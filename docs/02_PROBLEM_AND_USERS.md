# Problem and Users

## Central problem statement

Students miss essential learning instructions because information is spread across notebooks, verbal announcements, group chats, and separate school channels. Absence makes this worse. Parents cannot reliably tell what their child missed, and teachers repeat the same information individually.

This is a communication and learning-continuity problem, not a lack-of-content or marks-management problem.

## Primary user: teacher

**Need:** share a class update quickly, without formatting a long message several times.

**Pain today:** explains again to absent students/parents; group messages get lost; cannot tell whether a family saw an important item.

**Outcome:** publish once to the right class and see acknowledgement status.

## Primary beneficiary: student

**Need:** know exactly what to do today and what to catch up after being absent.

**Pain today:** depends on memory, friends, notebooks, or unclear messages.

**Outcome:** a short priority list with subject, task, due date, resource, and one clear action.

## Supporting user: parent/guardian

**Need:** help their child without guessing or burdening teachers.

**Pain today:** receives scattered messages and has no simple confirmation that the child saw work.

**Outcome:** an accurate child-specific view and acknowledgement.

## Customer/decision maker: school coordinator or principal

**Need:** a simple, safe tool teachers will actually use, with a manageable rollout.

**Outcome:** fewer missed notices, less repeated follow-up, better home-school coordination.

## Non-users in MVP

Office staff, finance teams, counsellors, external tutors, and district administrators are not product roles in Release 1. A coordinator account exists only to set up demo/pilot data.

## Assumptions to validate in a pilot

- Teachers can spend about 30–60 seconds posting a typical update.
- Families will use a browser link or web app at least several times a week.
- Acknowledgement is useful but should remain optional for ordinary items.
- A school will nominate one staff coordinator for initial setup.

Interview at least 3 teachers, 5 students, and 5 parents before presenting these as facts. Ask them to describe their current process and test a clickable prototype; do not lead them toward the preferred answer.
