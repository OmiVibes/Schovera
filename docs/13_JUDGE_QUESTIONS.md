# Likely Judge Questions and Grounded Answers

## Product

**What problem does it solve?**  Important classroom instructions are easily lost across notebooks, verbal messages, and group chats—especially after absence. EduBridge makes one trusted class update visible to the student and parent.

**Why not WhatsApp?**  WhatsApp is fast but unstructured and messages disappear in conversation. EduBridge gives each update a subject, task, deadline, class permission, and acknowledgement record.

**Why not a school ERP/LMS?**  Those can be useful, but they are broad and expensive to adopt. EduBridge starts with one specific, daily pain point and a short teacher workflow.

**What is innovative?**  The innovation is a focused school-to-home communication loop, not a claim that no school platform exists. One update becomes a structured, verifiable record for teacher, linked parent, and principal coverage.

**Why is acknowledgement important?**  It helps a teacher know who may need a reminder. It does not prove homework completion or learning, and should not be used to punish families.

## Technical

**How does it work?**  A teacher enters an update for an assigned student. The website checks their role/class, saves it in a database, and shows it to linked parents. When a parent acknowledges, the database records it and the teacher/principal views update.

**How do you protect privacy?**  Secure accounts and database permission rules limit each person to the relevant class/child. MVP stores only minimal information; the competition demo uses fictional data.

**Does it use AI?**  No. AI does not improve the basic problem enough to justify added complexity or misleading claims. Future AI would need a specific validated need, clear input/process/output, and human oversight.

**Can it scale?**  The data model separates schools, users, classes, updates, and acknowledgements. A managed database/hosting service can support incremental growth, but large-scale claims require real load testing and pilots.

## Business

**Who pays?**  The school, because it gains a practical communication tool and should not charge children for basic access.

**How would you make money?**  Start with a limited school/class pilot, then offer an annual school subscription after validating usage and support costs. Pricing should be researched rather than invented.

**How will schools adopt it?**  Begin with one coordinator and one/few classes, import only minimal roster links, train teachers in under an hour, and measure whether updates are fast and families use them.

**What is next?**  Validate through pilot interviews, improve onboarding and reminders, consider multilingual support, then evaluate a mobile app after the web workflow proves useful.
